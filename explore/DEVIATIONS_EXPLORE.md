# Deviations from EXPLORE_PLAN.md and implementation notes

Written on 7 November 2026 at 18:08 UTC, after the sandbox smoke test and before any laptop run. The plan itself (sha256 8e829ba31c87...) is unchanged. Each item says what differs from the plan's wording, why, and what it changes.

## Deviations

1. **X1a: E1 and E2 are computed by streaming, not by phase0.eval_basis.** The plan says the measures are computed "as phase0.eval_basis". eval_basis takes the full reference trajectories, stored at every step (fem_cantilever.newmark_full keeps displacement, velocity and acceleration: 3 x 641 x n numbers per case). At h_max = 0.025 (about 267,000 degrees of freedom) that is about 4.1 GB per case, and phase1.cmd_e2 keeps all 10 cases at once. explore.stream_metrics runs the same full and reduced recursions and accumulates the same sums step by step, so nothing of size (steps x n) is stored. E3, which is not among the plan's measures, is not computed. E2 is accumulated as e^T (K Phi c - K u), with e = Phi c - u formed at each step; the expanded form c^T Kr c - 2 c^T (K Phi)^T u + u^T K u lost up to seven significant digits to cancellation in a sandbox test (relative error 6.6e-7 in the sum for modal truncation at h_max = 0.2, against at most 5.5e-11 with the form used). Check: at h_max = 0.2 both are computed on every held-out geometry and the largest relative difference is stored (check_stream_vs_eval_basis). In the sandbox it was 2.6e-11 over 17 bases and 10 cases (geometry 4.0451_0.1017).

2. **X1a check at h_max = 0.2: how the relative difference of the Ritz errors is measured.** The plan reports any "relative difference above 1e-8 in any Ritz error". Two numbers are stored: (a) the largest absolute difference of the errors e_i, which is the relative difference of the Ritz frequencies because each e_i is itself a relative frequency error (check_ritz_vs_formal_abs); (b) the largest relative difference of the e_i themselves where the formal |e_i| exceeds 1e-8 (check_ritz_vs_formal_rel). Where e_i is small, (b) is inflated by round-off in the frequencies: in the sandbox, a different machine from the formal run, (a) was at most 1.5e-10 while (b) reached 1.8e-7 for a frequency-regression error of 4e-6. Any value above 1e-8 in either is reported and explained before the rest is read, as the plan says.

## Additions

3. Further checks at h_max = 0.2, stored with the results: the finite element frequencies against the formal record (check_w_vs_formal), E1 and E2 against the formal record (check_metrics_vs_formal), and any basis missing from the formal record.
4. The launcher refuses to start if any formal code file differs from formal/STAMP.txt, or if any formal network or evaluation record that the runs read differs from formal/results/SHA256SUMS_formal_results.txt.

## Implementation notes (no deviation)

5. The factorisations of the step operator and of M are computed once per geometry and mesh, instead of once per case as in newmark_full; the factors are the same.
6. X1b: the network basis is timed through basis_args, a compiled function that takes the mesh data as arguments, so that the compilation measured is that of the network rather than of the mesh arrays built into the program as constants. It equals lb.orthonormal_basis to a relative 1.4e-13 in the sandbox, and the difference is stored for every row (net_args_vs_lb). Full and reduced steps are timed as complete Newmark steps (right-hand side, solve, and the acceleration and velocity updates, as in newmark_full and reduced_rollout) on the tip pulse case: the median over 20 or 1,000 consecutive steps, then the median of three repeats. The Phase 0 cost table timed the right-hand side and the solve only.
7. The four FINE jobs run one after another. Peak memory of the set-up at h_max = 0.025 in the sandbox (mesh, 16 eigenpairs, factorisation, network inputs): about 3 GB. The order of the jobs only serves memory and timing, so a job that fails twice does not hold back the jobs after it.
8. X2 outputs are renamed from p1_* to x2_* after phase1.cmd_p1 has written them.
9. The processes are started with python -I -B, so that no bytecode files are written into formal/.

## Added on 7 November 2026 at 18:10 UTC, before any X3 computation

10. **X3 analysis, two additions.** (a) Besides the three shared O4 networks named in the plan, the shape-error analysis is also done for the supervised network (three seeds) and the mapped reduced basis, to see whether the relation between shape and frequency errors is particular to the trained networks or common to Rayleigh-Ritz approximations generally. These are reported separately from the planned O4 results. (b) "E2 against the eps_i" is made quantitative with a prediction that ignores coupling between modes: E2_pred^2 = sum over i <= 8 of p_i eps_i^2 + sum over i > 8 of p_i, where p_i is the share of the reference response's energy (summed over the time steps) in exact mode i, from the 16-mode modal rollout of the same case (exact for Rayleigh damping, because Newmark keeps the modes uncoupled; the first 16 modes are taken as the whole response). E2_pred is compared with the formal E2 of each evaluation case. Check: for modal truncation (eps_i = 0) it reduces to the energy above mode 8, which is compared with the formal E2 of modal_m8.

## Added on 7 November 2026 at 18:12 UTC, after the first X3 computation

11. **X3, E2 prediction: shares of the whole response's energy instead of the first 16 modes'.** Prompted by the result of the check in item 10: for modal truncation, the prediction from the energy in modes 9 to 16 was 0.78 times the formal E2 of modal_m8 (range 0.74 to 0.82), so the energy above mode 16 is not negligible against the truncation error, although it is negligible against the response (median share of modes 9 to 16: 1.1e-4). The energy of the whole reference response, summed over the time steps, is now computed directly by the full Newmark recursion, and p_i = (energy in exact mode i) / (whole energy). The prediction becomes E2_pred^2 = sum over i <= 8 of p_i eps_i^2 + (1 - sum over i <= 8 of p_i), and the modal check becomes an identity (E2 of modal_m8 = sqrt(1 - sum over i <= 8 of p_i)), which tests the computation of the p_i. The first prediction (item 10, 16 modes) is kept in the output as E2_pred16 and reported next to the new one; the first results were: E2_pred16 / E2 median 0.967 for the O4 networks (10 to 90% range 0.88 to 0.98).

## Added on 7 November 2026 at 18:23 UTC, after figure 5 was drawn

12. **X3, second prediction including the errors of the modal amplitudes over time.** Prompted by figure 5: the prediction of item 11 falls below 0.85 of the formal E2 in 28 of the 480 O4 cases. Those cases carry a large share of the response in mode 2 (median share 0.26 against 0.03 over all cases; 25 of the 28 are the evaluation cases with index 2, 7 and 8, counting from 0, where 8 is the tip pulse; all 28 are on the five held-out geometries with the notch at x_c above 3.9). The prediction of item 11 ignores that the reduced model's modal amplitudes differ from the exact ones over time, mainly through the frequency error, which accumulates as a phase error over the about 12 periods of mode 2 in the simulated window. Within the span of a basis, the Galerkin reduced model is uncoupled in the Ritz vectors, so its amplitudes q~_i(t) come from the reduced rollout on the Ritz vectors; the exact amplitudes q_i(t) from the 16-mode modal rollout. With vt_i = s_i v_i + D_i, the error in mode i is (s_i q~_i - q_i) v_i + q~_i D_i, whose energy summed over the steps is lambda_i sum (s_i q~_i - q_i)^2 (amplitude and phase) + lambda_i eps_i^2 sum q~_i^2 (shape) + 2 (D_i^T K v_i) sum (s_i q~_i - q_i) q~_i (cross term). E2_pred2 adds these over i <= 8 with the energy above mode 8, still ignoring coupling between different modes, and is reported with the share of each part. Both predictions stay in the output.

## Added on 8 November 2026 at 00:09 UTC, before any computation for it (approved by the author at 00:08 UTC)

18. **X1a control: mapped reduced basis with its sources on the evaluation mesh.** Purpose: to test whether the loss of accuracy of the mapped reduced basis on refined meshes (section 1.1 of the results) comes from its sources being computed at h_max 0.2, and to measure what fine sources cost offline. Design: for h_max 0.1 and 0.05, the 24 training geometries are built at that h_max and their 8 eigenpairs computed (phase0d.sources with the mesh size as a parameter; same src_prep, rbm_basis and Gram tolerance 1e-13); the basis (rbm_fine) is then mapped onto each of the 16 held-out geometries at the same h_max and evaluated as in X1a (geom_setup_h, the 10 evaluation cases, streaming E1 and E2, Ritz errors, sin^2, reproduced drop). Cross-check: on two held-out geometries per h_max the basis with the h_max 0.2 sources (rbm_all) is recomputed in the sandbox and compared with the laptop values of X1a. Timing: the 24 source preparations at 0.2, 0.1 and 0.05 and the mapping per geometry, timed in the same sandbox process (sandbox times, not comparable with the laptop times of X1b; only their ratios are used). Compared with the laptop X1a values of the networks and of rbm_all at the same h_max. Run in the sandbox, two processes, after a smoke test of every path. Expectation, written before running: with sources on the evaluation mesh the mapped reduced basis returns to about its accuracy at h_max 0.2 (largest frequency error near 7e-4, E2 near 0.04), and the offline cost grows like the eigen-solves. Reported whatever is found.

## Added on 8 November 2026 at 00:18 UTC, after the first attempt of item 18 failed, before the second

19. **Item 18: a destination node that lands on a source node at the notch corner.** All four first-attempt runs (h_max 0.1 and 0.05, two parts each, started 00:10 UTC) stopped at their first geometry on the assertion in phase0d.interp_mapped that every mapped point gets a finite value. Cause, found by a direct test: with sources at h_max 0.1, a node of held-out geometry 3.3505_0.1723 maps exactly onto the top corner of the notch slot of training geometry 1.5474_0.2566, at (1.5974, 1.0); scipy's LinearNDInterpolator finds a simplex there but returns NaN (a degenerate triangle of the Delaunay triangulation of the source nodes, which spans the notch void). With the sources at h_max 0.2 this never happened in X1a. Change, in rbfine.py only (the formal code is not changed): a copy of interp_mapped and rbm_basis (mass Gram, as used) in which any mapped point with a non-finite value takes the value of the nearest source node; the number of such points and their largest distance to that node are recorded for every geometry. The comparison basis with sources at 0.2 still uses phase0d.rbm_basis unchanged. Nothing else changes; the first attempt wrote no results.

## Added on 8 November 2026 at 00:46 UTC, after part 1 of the second attempt failed, before rerunning it

20. **Item 18: the same failure in the cross-check basis.** In the second attempt (started 00:20 UTC), part 0 ran normally (h_max 0.1 finished; 0.05 still running and left as it is), but both runs of part 1 stopped at their first geometry, now in the cross-check basis with the h_max 0.2 sources, which uses phase0d.rbm_basis unchanged: the same assertion as in item 19. On the laptop in X1a the same mapping onto the same geometries at h_max 0.1 and 0.05 gave finite values everywhere, so whether a mapped point lands on a degenerate simplex depends on the platform (mesh generator or Qhull build); the formal mapped-basis code is fragile at such coincidences, although it never failed in the formal runs or in X1a. Change, in rbfine.py only: the cross-check basis also uses rbm_basis_safe (item 19), which is identical to phase0d.rbm_basis when no point is non-finite (checked: bitwise equal bases on 3.3505_0.1723 at h_max 0.1) and records how many points needed the nearest-node value. Part 1 is rerun at both mesh sizes; part 0 keeps its results, made with phase0d.rbm_basis for the cross-check.

## Added on 8 November 2026 at 00:57 UTC, after reading the h_max 0.1 results of the second attempt, before a third attempt

21. **Item 19 fallback replaced: re-interpolate just inside the domain instead of taking the nearest node.** The h_max 0.1 results of the second attempt showed that the points with a non-finite value are not only exact coincidences with a source node: 1 to 5 points per geometry (over the 24 sources), most on the bottom edge near the free end (x about 9.96, y = 0), where scipy finds a degenerate simplex; their nearest source node is up to 0.038 away, so the nearest-node value of item 19 is a poor stand-in. Its effect showed in the cross-check: on 2.6263_0.3995 at h_max 0.1, one such point (absent on the laptop) moved the Ritz errors of the basis with the 0.2 sources by up to 2.4e-5 and E2 by 0.4%, against round-off (5.9e-13) on 3.3505_0.1723 where no point needed it. Change, in rbfine.py only: a mapped point with a non-finite value is moved by 1e-9 towards the centre of the beam (5, 0.5) and interpolated linearly again (the linear interpolant is continuous, so this gives its boundary value to within about 1e-9 of the field gradient); only if that is still non-finite does it take the nearest-node value. Counts of both kinds are recorded. The second attempt was stopped at 00:57 UTC (part 0 at h_max 0.05 had finished 6 of its 8 geometries; part 1 had finished h_max 0.1 and started 0.05); its outputs are kept in rbfine/attempt2 and not used. Both mesh sizes and both parts are rerun.
