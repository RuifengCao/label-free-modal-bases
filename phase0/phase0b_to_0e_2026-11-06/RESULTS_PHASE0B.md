# Phase 0b to 0e (exploratory): results

6 November 2026. Plans: PHASE0B_PLAN.md, PHASE0C_PLAN.md, PHASE0D_PLAN.md, PHASE0E_PLAN.md (hashes in PLAN_HASH.txt, each written before its runs). Deviations: DEVIATIONS_PHASE0.md, entries 4 to 7 and the note on check A5. Tables: TABLES_PHASE0B.md (generated by tables0b.py from the JSON outputs). No formal geometry was trained on or evaluated, except in smoke tests whose numbers were not read.

## 1. What ran

- Phase 0b: pilot 2, the formal Part II design on other geometries (24 training geometries, Sobol seed 31; 16 held out, seed 32; 13 of 16 inside the training hull). O4 and O3s with the shared network, 60,000 iterations, seed 0; evaluation on all 16 held-out geometries with modal truncation and with the reduced-basis control (l) (training eigenvectors interpolated in physical coordinates, k = 1, 4, all); the same control on the Phase 0 pilot; the threshold rule. Checks A5 (O3s against a loop form: value and gradient within 4e-13) and A6 (the control reproduces the Phase 0 nearest-geometry numbers within 9e-10) passed.
- Phase 0d: two classical controls that do not have the defect of (l): a reduced basis from training eigenvectors carried over by a notch-aligning map (m), and a regression of the frequencies over the two parameters (n). Checks B1 (map round trip, 1.8e-15) and B2 (self-reproduction, at most 2.0e-11) passed; B4 exposed a truncation floor (deviation 4).
- Phase 0c: one network per mode on G0 (20,000 iterations, seeds 0 and 1; the O4 seed-0 run was stopped a minute in and rerun from scratch, deviation 7).
- Phase 0e: one network per mode on pilot 2, against the shared network at the same number of iterations (30,000; deviations 5 and 6).

## 2. Thresholds of H4 to H6

Pilot 2, O4, shared network: H4 measure 0.13% (rule: keep 1% if at most 0.67%), H5 measure 96.4% (keep 90% if at least 93.3%), H6 measure 6.2% (keep 10% if at most 6.7%). All three thresholds stay as planned: H4 <= 1%, H5 >= 90%, H6 <= 10%. H7's direction was seen (median first-frequency error 46.1% for O3s against 0.18% for O4).

Margins differ. H4 has a factor of about 8 (the largest per-geometry H4 value is 0.78%); it is a floor of usefulness rather than a severe test. H5 has 6 points (12 of 16 geometries above 90%; two of the four below have finite element drops under 1%, where the ratio is unstable; over the 14 geometries with drops of at least 1% the median is 96.5%). H6 has a factor of 1.6 (median 5.5% inside the hull; 7.8%, 9.9% and 16.4% for the three outside), and seed-to-seed variation on the family has not been measured, so a seed above 10% in the formal runs is a real possibility.

## 3. Classical controls

(l), training eigenvectors interpolated in physical coordinates, fails: with all 24 training geometries the largest frequency error per geometry has a median of 4.9% and reaches 15.7%, the H6 measure is 39%, and the H5 measure 12%. Each snapshot carries the kink of its own notch at its own position, where the held-out geometry has a full section, so the compliance at the new notch cannot be represented. It is not a fair stand-in for classical practice.

(m), the same eigenvectors carried over by a map that aligns the notches (translation in a zone of half-width 0.5 around the notch, a two-piece stretch in depth inside it, blended to the identity over a further 0.5; parameters fixed before running), with all 24 training geometries and Gram truncation 1e-13 (deviation 4): H4 measure 0.024%, H5 measure 97.9%, H6 measure 3.9%, median largest error 0.07%, largest 3.05%. It is more accurate than the O4 network on 13 of the 16 held-out geometries by the largest frequency error and on 12 by E2. It is less accurate on the geometry farthest outside the training range (x_c = 4.995, d = 0.393: 3.05% against 1.83%, E2 29% against 16%, reproduced drop 31% against 82%) and on two shallow notches (d = 0.10 and 0.12). With the planned truncation (1e-10) it is less accurate (H6 measure 5.8%, largest error 4.07%) because the truncation drops directions that are small in the mass norm but not in the energy norm. On the Phase 0 pilot (8 training geometries, 3 of 4 held-out geometries outside the hull) it is level with the network (H4 0.11%, H5 83%, H6 16%, against 0.61%, 87%, 15%).

(n), thin-plate-spline interpolation of each frequency over the normalised parameters: median absolute first-frequency error 0.02%, H5 measure 100.2%, largest error 2.2%. It gives frequencies only.

So on this two-parameter family the network is not more accurate than classical methods that use the eigen-solutions of the training geometries. What separates it is that its training needs no eigen-solution at all (only K and M of each training geometry), and that it degraded less on the one geometry far outside the training range (weak evidence).

## 4. One network per mode

G0, Phase 0c (20,000 iterations, seeds 0 and 1; TABLES_PHASE0B.md section 3; evaluation in out0c/eval0c.json). With one network per mode, O3's first-frequency error fell from 3.66% and 3.01% (shared network, seeds 0 and 1; 5.11% for seed 2) to 0.37% and 0.25%, ten times or more; its other modes stayed below 0.33%. O3s behaved like O3 (first-frequency error 0.41% and 0.27%, largest error 0.41% and 0.32%). O2 did not improve: 26.4% and 22.4% against 12.0% and 17.2%, with E2 of 125% and 117%. O4 learnt all eight in both architectures (largest error 0.12% and 0.11% with one network per mode, against 0.08% to 0.10% shared; E2 4.3% and 4.4%). This is what PHASE0C_PLAN.md expected before the runs: O3 and O3s no longer leave mode 1 behind, O2 still does, O4 learns all eight.

The reading: with the earlier columns stopped, O3 makes each column answer only for its own Rayleigh quotient. With separate parameters per mode, Adam scales each mode's gradient on its own, so the eigenvalue weighting no longer starves mode 1; with shared parameters the gradient is dominated by the stiff modes. O2 couples all columns through the orthonormalisation, which separate networks cannot undo. O4 weights the modes equally, so there is nothing for separate scaling to correct. The weighting acts through shared parameters.

Family, Phase 0e (pilot 2, seed 0, 30,000 iterations for all four runs; TABLES_PHASE0B.md section 4, with the 60,000-iteration shared runs of Phase 0b in section 1):

- Shared O3s fails at both lengths: median first-frequency error 99% at 30,000 iterations and 46% at 60,000, with excursions above 1,000% on the training geometries in between.
- Per-mode O3s does not fail: median first-frequency error 0.71%, H4 measure 0.56%; but it stays behind O4 (H5 measure 84.5%, H6 measure 13.3%, both outside the formal thresholds).
- O4 is the same in both architectures at equal iterations (median first-frequency error 0.27% shared, 0.29% per mode; H6 measure 7.7% and 8.0%; largest error 1.91% and 3.04%), and the per-mode run takes 1.8 times as long (3,807 s against 2,095 s). At equal time (about 3,800 s) the shared network at 60,000 iterations is better (0.18%, 6.2%).

So the direction of H7 (O3s behind O4) holds in both architectures; the collapse of shared O3s comes from the per-geometry sorting together with shared parameters. One network per mode costs about twice as much per iteration (127 to 155 ms against 63 to 89 ms in the sandbox, depending on the objective and the run).

## 5. Consequences for the pre-registration

Proposed to Ruifeng; nothing below is fixed until he approves and the files are stamped.

- Thresholds of H4 to H6: as planned (1%, 90%, 10%), by the rule.
- Part II controls: the mapped reduced basis with all 24 training geometries (truncation 1e-13) and the frequency regression replace control (l); they are reported whatever they show and carry no hypothesis; they are grouped with the supervised network as methods that use eigen-solutions of the training geometries. Control (l) is reported only here, as the reason for the map.
- Part I: add one network per mode for O2, O3 and O4 on G1 to G3 with seeds 10 and 11 (18 runs), with three predictions: H8, per-mode O3's first-frequency error below a third of shared O3's on the same geometry and seed (G0: ratios of about 10 and 12); H9, per-mode O2 still leaves mode 1 behind (e_1 > 1% and above e_3..e_8; G0: 26.4% and 22.4%); H10, per-mode O4 learns all eight (largest error at most 0.5%; G0: 0.12% and 0.11%).
- Part II: add one network per mode for O4 and O3s, seeds 0 to 2, 30,000 iterations (about the training time of 60,000 shared iterations), with H7sep: per-mode O3s behind per-mode O4 in the median first-frequency error (pilot 2: 0.71% against 0.29%).
- Claims: the weighting result for O2 and O3 is stated for networks whose modes share parameters; Part II does not claim more accuracy than classical methods that use eigen-solutions.
- Run time on the laptop: about 4 hours before these additions, about 8 hours with them (sandbox timings scaled by the laptop measurements of 6 November).
